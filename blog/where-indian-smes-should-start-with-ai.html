Where an Indian small business should start with AI

MM
Mamta MorFounder, Seemara · 24 November 2026 · 6 min read
In short

Don't start with an AI strategy. Start with one workflow that happens every day, repeats the same steps, uses information you already have, and has a number you can watch. Build a small working version, connect it to your tools, and expand only once it pays for itself.

Owners of small businesses ask us the same question in different words: where do we even start with AI? The honest answer is smaller than most people expect. You don't need a strategy deck. You need one workflow.

The test for a good first workflow

A good first AI project usually passes five checks:

  1. It happens often. Daily or many times a week, so the time saved adds up quickly.
  2. It repeats the same steps. Answering the same questions, sending the same reminders, writing the same kind of caption.
  3. The information already exists. In your catalog, price list, policies, past chats or spreadsheets.
  4. You can measure it. Response time, leads followed up, hours saved, creatives shipped.
  5. A mistake is cheap to catch. A person can review the output before it matters.

Four places most SMEs start

1. Lead follow-up

Enquiries arrive on WhatsApp, Instagram and the website, and some never get a reply. An agent that collects requirements, answers the basics and reminds your team to follow up is often the fastest win. We wrote a separate guide to WhatsApp lead agents.

2. Repeated customer questions

Timings, prices, delivery, appointment slots, return policy. A support agent grounded in your own information answers these consistently and hands the unusual ones to a person.

3. Weekly marketing creative

If someone spends every week writing captions and briefing designers, a marketing engine that knows your brand can produce first drafts in minutes. That's the idea behind Kaizen.

4. Internal knowledge

New staff keep asking the same experienced people the same things. A knowledge agent built on your SOPs and training material gives everyone one place to ask.

What not to start with

  • Anything where a wrong answer has legal, medical or financial consequences and no one reviews it.
  • A workflow nobody owns. If no one is responsible for the result, no one will improve the system.
  • A "replace the whole team" plan. The best first projects remove the boring part of a job, not the job.

What the first few weeks should look like

Map the workflow as it really happens today. Build a small prototype and use it on real cases. Connect it to the tools your team already uses, such as WhatsApp, a sheet or your CRM. Then watch the one number you chose and improve from feedback. If the number moves, expand. If it doesn't, you've learned cheaply.

At Seemara this is exactly how we work: one painful workflow, a working version early, then growth. If you're unsure which workflow to pick, email mamta@seemara.com with a few lines about your business.

Questions

Do we need a tech team to start with AI?

No. A good first project uses tools you already have, such as WhatsApp, spreadsheets or your CRM. You need someone who owns the workflow, not a developer.

How much data do we need?

Usually less than you think: your catalog or price list, policies, common questions and some past conversations are enough for a first version.
